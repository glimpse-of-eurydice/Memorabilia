# Street trees and urban heat

Street trees can reduce pedestrian heat exposure in two related ways. Their
canopies block part of the incoming solar radiation, creating shade over
pavement and people. Leaves also release water through evapotranspiration,
which can cool nearby air when sufficient water is available.

The benefit is conditional rather than automatic. A tree that shades a busy
walkway may reduce direct heat exposure even when its effect on neighborhood
air temperature is modest. In a water-scarce place, however, poorly selected
species or irrigation-intensive planting can create maintenance and water
costs that complicate the cooling benefit. Placement, canopy growth, local
climate, and long-term care therefore affect whether planting produces useful
cooling.
